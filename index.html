02Work

Four pieces of work that show how I operate.

Problem
The group rebranded to Edustep. New name, no brand awareness, no search presence, and direct enrolments arriving organically at a trickle while nine partner colleges needed filling.
What I did
Took the lead on every marketing activity. Built paid campaigns on tightly capped daily budgets and ran them for weeks to months rather than in bursts, so results were readable. Tracked performance by channel and moved spend to what converted. Paired that with SEO, email and landing pages.
Outcome
Monthly direct enrolments rose roughly tenfold on a modest annual advertising budget, while the portfolio grew from three partner colleges to nine managed at once.
Problem
Upskroll is our own student management system for Australian RTOs, entering a market with established incumbents and buyers who change systems reluctantly.
What I did
Wrote a 90-day sales plan. Chose personalised outreach to more than 60 training providers over a mass campaign, because this buyer responds to someone who understands their compliance load. Set a capped advertising budget so spend stayed proportional to a pre-launch product.
Outcome
Upskroll is live, with a soft launch in December 2026 and a pipeline built before release.
Problem
The group paid recurring subscriptions for cloud storage and other tooling, with company files sitting on third-party infrastructure we did not control.
What I did
Set an internal-first rule: every product is used by our own staff on real work before any public release, so problems surface early and release quality stays high. Took rs-drive from idea to internal use, then rs-conference, with rs-mail in final development and rs-auth giving a single sign-in across all of them.
Outcome
Third-party cloud storage subscriptions dropped, a recurring cost removed and company files kept in-house. Five products now in the range, built by about ten people across two countries.
Problem
Short-supply products with three to six week replenishment, and several of Australia’s largest retailers all wanting them. Release stock too freely to one account and the rest of the market runs dry.
What I did
Owned the exclusion list and judged allocation customer by customer. Reconciled daily forecasts from SAP and from the retailers’ own systems. After six months, designed new Excel reporting that pulled about five separate reports into one view, built to feed a Power BI dashboard directly.
Outcome
Daily release decisions made without leaving the market short, and forecast visibility that planners across several teams worked from.
